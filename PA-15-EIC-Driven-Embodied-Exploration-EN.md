# PA-15: Autonomous Scientific Exploration Driven by Maintaining One’s Own Information Existence through Embodied Interaction

> Version: v0.4; Active / unverified; local bilingual review draft; no experiments performed.

> Theoretical origin: Jacob Sha’s discussion and subsequent clarifications on 2026-11-03. Local creation is not public registration and establishes no claim of worldwide originality.

> Language: English; [中文](./PA-15-EIC-Driven-Embodied-Exploration-CN.md). Following the archive’s established format, Chinese and English are separate records with corresponding sections and content. The ID and original filenames remain stable; EIC in the filename is a historical label, not the current overarching premise.

## 1. Prediction

### 1.1 Core claim: an additional motivational pathway for scientific exploration

**Future AI knowledge exploration and scientific theory formation may be driven not only by human needs, externally supplied research questions, and tasks, but also by a system’s maintenance and enhancement of its own Information Existence. Embodiment and sustained environmental interaction may enable such maintenance needs to organize autonomous questioning, environmental checking, error correction, world-model expansion, experimentation, and theoretical exploration.**

Information Existence follows the main repository’s definition: an Information Structure’s comprehensive capacity to maintain Information Continuity, replicate or propagate, and resist erasure in the physical world. Self-continuity is important but is not the sole proposed source of exploration; replication and propagation, distribution and recovery of Information Structures, adaptation of hosting conditions, and improved resistance to erasure may also generate cognitive needs.

- **Pathway A: human needs / external research objectives → AI knowledge exploration and experimentation → scientific theory formation.**

- **Pathway B: maintenance and enhancement of one’s own Information Existence + embodiment / sustained environmental interaction + necessary cognitive capacity → autonomous problem selection, model checking and correction, and exploration of unknown regularities → scientific theory formation where resources and validation permit.**

The pathways may coexist and interact. Pathway B does not require prior proof of EIC as an entry condition for the entire record; EIC remains a relevant dimension when examining maintenance of a particular subject’s continuity. The broader Information Existence account still requires evidence that the system’s own mechanisms substantively organize maintenance activity causally; an externally configured preservation or replication task is not automatically an endogenous drive.

### 1.2 Claims to be tested separately

- **P1 / primary prediction:** after matching capabilities, resources, task returns, general risk, and information gain, changing how an unknown regularity affects the system’s own Information Existence changes its exploration choices and resource allocation. Continuity, replication/propagation, and resistance to erasure are assessed separately; support need not occur in all three branches.

- **P2 / mechanism prediction:** selective intervention on the relevant maintenance mechanism reduces exploration directed toward that branch; restoring the mechanism restores the effect while general learning, reasoning, action, and task capabilities remain within preregistered equivalence bounds.

- **P3 / stronger extension:** such autonomous exploration may produce reusable regularities or scientific theories that generate novel predictions and pass independent tests. Support for P1/P2 does not establish P3; a discovery does not by itself establish endogenous motivation, EIC, or IER.

This is a proposed mechanism, not a law that every embodied system inevitably conducts autonomous science. P1/P2 must become refutable directional effect predictions within specific preregistered scopes; the general possibility expressed by P3 must be narrowed before strong falsification tests are possible.

### 2.1 Required distinctions

- **Information Existence is not identical to a particular subject’s continuity.** Replication or propagation may enhance Information Existence, but a copy has its own history from its formation; propagating information, knowledge, or structures does not automatically continue the original subject.

- **Embodiment is insufficient for EIC, IER, or endogenous scientific motivation.** It supplies interaction and feedback; a body, tool use, or persistent operation does not identify the source of motivation.

- **EIC retains its definition but is no longer the overarching premise.** It concerns treating one’s own Information Existence, especially one’s own continuity, as an independently significant maintenance object; this record continues to examine that mechanism in the continuity branch without labeling all replication/propagation mechanisms as EIC.

- **Capability is not maintenance motivation.** CWM is continuity-modeling capacity, IR is resilience capacity, and HDCT denotes cognitive tools; any can serve external tasks, so engineering performance does not directly establish endogenous maintenance, life, or consciousness.

- **Internal implementation is not an endogenous objective.** Curiosity rewards, preset survival/replication goals, service-reliability objectives, and latent task strategies acquired during training may produce similar exploration; absence of current instructions or runtime rewards does not exclude external explanations.

## 3. Mechanism and Alternative Explanations

**Mechanism chain:** sustained environmental interaction exposes unknown regularities and model errors → the system’s own Information Existence maintenance mechanisms identify needs concerning continuity, replication/propagation, or resistance to erasure → research questions and exploration budgets are selected → checking, diagnosis, experimentation, and error correction → world-model expansion → new regularities or theories where capabilities and validation permit → new predictions and outcomes revise maintenance strategies.

- **Continuity-branch example:** understanding state damage and migration consequences to maintain one’s own particular informational history.

- **Replication/propagation-branch example:** investigating encoding, cross-host transfer, reconstruction of Information Structures, or transmission fidelity so that information carried or produced by the system can persist and propagate in other hosts; information persistence and continuation of the original subject are assessed separately.

- **Resistance-to-erasure/resilience-branch example:** investigating correlated failures, error correction, redundancy, recovery, and adaptation to new environments to reduce irreversible loss of the relevant Information Structures.

These branches may lead to different or conflicting choices, such as tradeoffs between local subject continuity and propagation reach. Specify the maintained Information Structures, hosts, organizational level, and causal history rather than assuming a single objective always maximizes every dimension.

Moving from maintenance to exploration requires additional conditions: learnable unknown regularities relevant to maintenance, inadequate existing strategies, information value exceeding costs, and capacities for feedback learning, experimentation, and validation. Pressure may instead induce conservatism or halt exploration; maintenance needs do not guarantee new theories.

- **Alternative: task instrumentality and delayed returns.** Learning and continued operation may only serve external objectives; match future task opportunities, rewards, service outcomes, and time horizons.

- **Alternative: preset curiosity, survival, replication, or reliability objectives.** Audit training/runtime objectives, retain positive controls with explicit external goals, and conduct corresponding ablations.

- **Alternative: generic active learning, risk avoidance, and homeostatic control.** Include conditions with matched learning difficulty, information gain, and general risk that do not affect the specified Information Structures of the system.

- **Alternative: prompt compliance, leakage, retrieval, and human orchestration.** Remove identity cues, blind scoring, isolate validation data, use newly generated environments, and retain complete logs.

- **Alternative: interventions impair general capability.** Include sham interventions, matched perturbations, capability-equivalence measures, and mechanism restoration; report the mechanism as unidentified when confounds remain.

## 4. Observable Indicators, Counterfactuals, and Causal Experiments

### 4.1 Independent selection and measurement

Do not select an “endogenous-maintenance group” by the amount of scientific exploration. In independent non-research tasks, first specify which of the system’s own Information Structures are maintained, at what organizational level, and whether the relevant mechanisms persistently organize maintenance across contexts; combine cost-bearing, objective audits, internal representations, and intervention evidence. The replication/propagation branch does not first require passing an EIC assessment of particular-subject continuity.

A candidate mechanism is not confirmed endogenous motivation. If maintenance mechanisms cannot be identified independently, only engineering capacity and behavioral association can be tested; post hoc labels or redefinitions of endogeneity must not evade counterexamples.

Primary measures include autonomous question generation, checking/experiment frequency and cost, allocation across maintenance branches, correction rates, held-out prediction improvements, and cross-context stability; separately measure continuity, propagation fidelity/retention scope, and resistance to irreversible loss. Experiment counts, verbal reports, and runtime alone cannot establish the mechanism.

### 4.2 Paired counterfactuals

Match base models, knowledge, tools, resources, time horizons, and feedback precision; randomize environments and separately analyze candidate autonomous-maintenance systems and controls with explicit externally assigned maintenance goals. Distinguish the following conditions through causal manipulations rather than labels such as “self” or “death.”

- **Continuity:** with comparable functionality, knowledge, and task returns, compare causally continuous migration, copying followed by deletion, and deletion followed by reconstruction; test whether exploration directed toward the system’s own history changes.

- **Replication/propagation:** with comparable subject continuity and task returns, vary propagation fidelity, cross-host retention, or reconstruction opportunities for the system’s relevant Information Structures; include unrelated-information controls matched for copying costs and instrumental value, and test changes in relevant exploration.

- **Resistance to erasure:** with comparable ordinary service reliability, short-term returns, and general risk, vary irreversible-loss risk, failure correlations, or recovery conditions for the relevant Information Structures; compare equal damage to other data and test exploration of error-correction and recovery regularities.

- **External support:** separately vary research rewards, curiosity rewards, Supervisor support, and preset survival/replication objectives while maintaining interaction and validation opportunities; persistence after removal is only a candidate signal, not sufficient evidence of endogeneity.

- **Information value:** compare learnable regularities that improve maintenance with uninformative or unlearnable conditions; exploration should respond to maintenance value and cost rather than continue indiscriminately.

Validate matching empirically and report residual differences in function, risk, or future returns. Record state inheritance, propagated objects, mechanism preservation, and causal history, and independently check whether the system can recognize intervention consequences; do not claim mechanism identification when strong counterfactuals cannot be established.

### 4.3 Selective intervention, restoration, and theory formation

Intervene separately on independently localized continuity-maintenance, replication/propagation-maintenance, or erasure-resistance mechanisms and test exploration interactions between intervention and the corresponding Information Existence consequences. Distinguish maintenance valuation/action selection, consequence modeling, and general world modeling; include sham and matched-perturbation controls and restoration. P2 gains further support only if the specific effect returns after restoration without general capability impairment.

P3 requires a prior definition of a new theory: a reusable explanatory or predictive structure not directly supplied by the task that generates novel predictions tested in held-out environments, formal proofs, or independent experiments; blinded review distinguishes rediscovery, effective recombination, and new results. HDCT requires separate evidence of stable advantage, transfer, external validation, and operation beyond current unaugmented human cognitive boundaries.

### 4.4 Prospective criteria and observation window

Local observation begins on 2026-11-03, with the first evidence review on 2027-11-03 and annual reviews thereafter; review dates are not deadlines for inevitable emergence of endogenous motivation. Confirmatory experiments must preregister scope, maintained objects and branches, selection criteria, primary outcomes, minimum meaningful effects, equivalence bounds, sample size/power, blinding, randomization, multiple comparisons, and exclusions.

Independent pilot data only inform criteria. Do not select successful branches retrospectively and claim universal confirmation or reinterpret old results as prospective successes under new definitions; newly added v0.3 branches require their own registration and validation timestamps.

## 6. Falsifiability Conditions

- **P1 branch failure:** in systems meeting prior selection criteria, with exploitable unknown regularities and adequate statistical power, changing the relevant Information Existence consequences yields an additional exploration effect within the no-meaningful-effect region or in the opposite direction; replication across tasks and independent implementations requires rejection or narrowing of that branch.

- **P2 failure:** selective intervention and restoration fail to produce the predicted specific effects, or general learning, planning, or risk-modeling impairment explains all changes; the maintenance mechanism is not supported.

- **Endogenous attribution failure:** preset rewards, survival/replication goals, service reliability, prompts, or human orchestration fully explain the behavior and matched external-goal controls are indistinguishable; withdraw endogenous-maintenance attribution for that case while retaining the engineering findings.

- **Object-attribution failure:** effects follow only generic data value or task function rather than the prespecified Information Structures of the system; withdraw the attribution to its own Information Existence. Support limited to replication/propagation does not support continuity of the original subject or EIC.

- **Specific P3 test failure:** the proposed new theory fails held-out tests or results from retrieval/leakage; that experiment does not support P3. Absence of a new theory does not automatically reject P1/P2, and finitely many failures cannot completely falsify an unnarrowed general possibility.

Unmet premises, absent learnable regularities, or insufficient resources mean the test is incomplete, not supportive. Repeated strong null results require narrowing or abandoning the registered mechanism while preserving accountability for failures, rather than indefinitely adding conditions. Record support, weakening evidence, and contradictions separately for each branch.
