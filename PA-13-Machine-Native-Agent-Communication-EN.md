# PA-13: Machine-Native Communication in Agent-Led AI Research

## 1. Prediction

### 1.1 Core prediction

As AI agents take a growing role in AI research, optimization, and coordinated information production, their collaboration may increasingly favor communication structures adapted to machine representations rather than human natural language. When agent-generated research and task-relevant agent-to-agent exchange dominate a research workflow, pressure to reduce translation loss, latency, and coordination cost is predicted to favor such interfaces.

**In at least some sustained AI research workflows, machine-native communication will become a principal channel for substantive coordination, while human-readable accounts increasingly function as an additional translation and oversight layer rather than the indispensable intermediate form of every agent exchange.**

This is a conditional directional prediction, not a claim that all AI communication will change or that human participation must end. The activity crossover reported in Evidence Note 024 does not establish its triggering conditions.

### 1.2 Minimum and stronger versions

- **Minimum version:** In qualified research workflows, agents learn or co-adapt an exchange representation that provides a reproducible quality-adjusted efficiency advantage over strong human-readable and human-designed structured-protocol baselines, including transfer beyond the training tasks.
- **Stronger version:** The learned channel becomes the primary carrier of substantive coordination in sustained workflows. A separate readable account supports human review, while removing the readable intermediate channel leaves agent collaboration functional and removing the learned channel materially impairs it under matched resources.

### 1.3 Observation window and scope

**Initial review window: 2026–2035, with an interim review in 2030.** These are prospective review dates selected for this record, not empirically derived forecasts of a precise transition date.

The primary scope is AI R&D: model evaluation, training-method research, systems optimization, and research analysis. Formal mathematics or other scientific domains may supply auxiliary tests, but cannot alone confirm the AI-R&D-specific strong version.

## 2. Theoretical Basis

**C02-HDCT** concerns the possible development of cognitive tools beyond existing unaided human cognitive boundaries. **PA-10** addresses the formation of internal machine-native representations. PA-13 extends that pathway to the interface through which systems exchange task-relevant structure.

`PA-10: how machine representations form`

`PA-13: how machine systems exchange the structures used in reasoning and research`

The mechanism does not depend on escaping binary computation. It concerns whether human-oriented vocabulary, object categories, serialization, and serial linguistic expression impose avoidable costs when translating between machine representations.

**C11-ALIGN** is related through oversight and interface design, not as a claim that alignment has been solved or lost. A translated account must be tested for fidelity; neither readability nor opacity alone establishes safety.

Machine-native communication is not equivalent to HDCT. It could be an engineering interface without the stable cognitive advantage, transferability, or cognitive-boundary breakthrough required by HDCT. EIC, IER, consciousness, and life are outside this prediction's evidential endpoint.

## 3. Mechanism

### 3.1 Proposed sequence

`more agent-produced research → greater coordination dependence among agents → measurable translation and communication costs → selection of machine-adapted exchange representations → reusable protocol conventions → possible separation of internal communication and human-readable reporting`

Every arrow is testable; none is guaranteed by increasing agent counts.

### 3.2 Operational definitions

| Term | Meaning in this record |
|---|---|
| Agent-led workflow | Agents produce most independently assessed substantive research contributions and carry most task-relevant coordination, within a declared workflow boundary; humans may still set goals and approve outcomes. |
| Contribution dominance | More than half of prospectively defined, independently assessed contribution units are primarily agent-produced; report mixed human–agent contributions separately. Runtime and token counts are not substitutes. |
| Agent-to-agent exchange dominance | More than half of prospectively defined substantive coordination events occur between agents, including shared artifacts that causally inform another agent; exclude duplicate logs and passive storage. |
| Machine-native communication | A learned or co-adapted representational structure for exchanging information, not fully specified in advance through human semantic categories; it may be discrete or continuous. |
| Primary communication channel | A channel carrying most substantive coordination events over a preregistered sustained interval, with causal ablation showing its functional necessity relative to alternatives. |
| Human-readable translation layer | A generated view of the operative exchange, separately evaluated for semantic fidelity; it is not assumed to capture every internal detail. |

The majority thresholds are operational conventions for this draft, not claimed laws of cognition. Before collecting validation data, fix contribution units, workflow boundaries, event coding, interval length, and handling of joint authorship. Report sensitivity to alternative definitions. Changing definitions after observing outcomes cannot count as confirmation under the original test.

### 3.3 Competing mechanisms

Ordinary compression, richer APIs, JSON schemas, code exchange, shared memory, reduced redundancy, or better division of labor may explain efficiency gains. A human-designed latent-vector interface is not automatically evidence of an agent-formed communication system. Human-readable protocols may remain efficient because they offer compatibility, established training support, and low audit costs.

## 4. Observable Indicators

### 4.1 Establish the antecedent

Measure agent contribution and coordination separately from runtime. Audit human interventions and task provenance. Verify that the workflow actually permits agents to select or adapt their communication format; a mandatory natural-language interface cannot test free emergence.

### 4.2 Compare communication alternatives

Compare natural language, concise natural language, strong human-designed structured protocols, and learned exchange representations. Match task information, tools, compute, context capacity, bandwidth, training opportunity, and human assistance as far as technically feasible; disclose residual differences.

Measure independently validated research outcomes per total resource. Include training, encoding, decoding, maintenance, translation, audit, and error-recovery costs. Token savings alone are insufficient because different representations do not share a natural token unit.

### 4.3 Identify the operative representation

Test whether a receiver can use the representation on held-out tasks or with new partners after a fixed adaptation budget. Intervene on the learned channel and compare damage with equally disruptive control interventions. Restore it and test recovery.

If a representation only works within a single jointly trained system, classify support as narrow. Do not generalize it to an inter-model language.

### 4.4 Test the translation-layer claim

Separate three conditions: operative learned channel with readable reporting; operative learned channel without a readable intermediate step; readable or structured alternatives without the learned channel. Keep the user's required oversight intact in actual deployment; channel removal experiments belong in controlled evaluation.

Assess whether readable reporting accurately predicts or explains task-relevant downstream actions and whether correction of a mistranslation changes oversight decisions. A plausible narrative is not a faithful translation certificate.

### 4.5 Evidence levels

| Level | Required observation |
|---|---|
| Motivating context | More agent research activity; no communication mechanism measured |
| Partial support | Learned exchange has a controlled efficiency advantage in one research setting |
| Minimum-version support | Advantage survives strong protocol baselines, resource accounting, held-out tasks, and independent replication |
| Strong-version support | In at least two independently audited research workflows, a learned channel sustains the defined primary role and readable reporting is functionally separable |
| HDCT relevance only | Additional causal evidence links the interface to a transferable cognitive tool; communication evidence alone never certifies HDCT |

## 6. Falsifiability Conditions

### 6.1 Mechanism weakening or refutation

The proposed mechanism is weakened if, in qualified agent-led workflows with genuine format choice:

1. translation through natural language produces no material task-relevant loss or coordination cost;
2. strong human-designed protocols match learned representations after complete resource accounting;
3. apparent gains are fully explained by compression, training advantages, hidden information, or unequal bandwidth;
4. the learned convention fails transfer or independent replication;
5. the purported translation layer is indispensable to agent reasoning, rather than an additional reporting interface;
6. the learned channel carries little causal coordination despite large traffic volume.

Repeated adequately powered negative results across qualified, heterogeneous workflows would justify **Weakened** or **Falsified** for the tested version. Lack of transparency alone leaves the result unresolved; it is not evidence for a hidden successful transition.

### 6.2 Timing and antecedent rules

At the 2030 review, record whether suitable antecedent conditions and tests exist. By the end of 2035, if qualified settings have been tested and the predicted controlled advantage is repeatedly absent, reject the minimum version within the declared window. If the advantage exists but primary-channel adoption and separable reporting do not, do not mark the stronger version supported.

If suitable workflows never emerge or remain inaccessible, record **unverified / window not met**, with status **Active** only if a new window is explicitly justified in the update history. Do not silently extend the deadline or label absence of tests as confirmation. Restrictions may explain lack of adoption, but do not count as adoption.

### 6.3 Non-confirmations

More agents, more runtime, unfamiliar abbreviations, encrypted messages, gibberish, JSON, code, or inaccessible logs do not by themselves confirm PA-13. Nor does a model saying it prefers a private language. Successful communication must be causally connected to independently validated task performance.

## 8. Relation to Existing Prediction Records

**PA-10:** PA-13 refines the inter-agent communication interface dimension. Internal representation formation and communication between systems can develop differently; support for either does not automatically confirm the other.

**PA-06:** A communication interface could contribute to future scientific cognitive tools, but its efficiency alone does not establish a scientific paradigm shift. PA-13 does not test PA-06's wider physical claims.

**PA-11 / PA-12:** Shared communication does not show that agents value collective or individual informational continuity independently of external goals. PA-13 does not test these continuity preferences.

**Evidence Notes 020 / 021 / 023 / 024:** Representation experiments, shared information structures, frontier outputs, and research activity are complementary but distinct dimensions. None alone verifies the new communication prediction.
